# Porting Apidya to MSX: a guide

This is a plan and a data reference for someone who wants to build an MSX version of *Apidya*
(Kaiko / Play Byte, Amiga, 1992). **No MSX code exists yet.** What does exist is a complete, playable
reverse-engineered reimplementation (this repository, GDScript) and a large set of research notes, which
together answer most of the "what does the original do" questions an MSX programmer would otherwise have
to find out with a disassembler.

Everything below that talks about MSX hardware is written from the point of view of what the *game* needs;
check the details against the V9938 / V9958 data books and your assembler's documentation before you rely on
them.

## 1. Ground rules

* **Nothing from the game may be distributed.** Do what this project does: ship a converter, not assets. The
  converter runs on the user's machine against their own `Disk.1` / `Disk.2` (WHDLoad install of the Amiga
  game) and produces the MSX ROM / disk image.
* The Amiga game is a 68000 program. There is **no practical way to run it on a Z80** (a 7 MHz 68000 interpreted
  by a 3.58 MHz Z80 is two orders of magnitude too slow). The MSX version must be a rewrite of the engine that
  consumes converted data. That is also what the GDScript port started as, and its first version (the native
  `src/game/handlers_l1.gd`) is a good example of how big a hand port of the enemy handlers is.
* The Godot port is your **reference implementation and test oracle** (section 11).

## 2. What you get from this project

| `src/game/` | The engine rules in readable form: `game.gd` (loop, waves, sections, lives, difficulty), `player_state.gd` (power-up meter), `weapons.gd` (shots, bombs, charge blast), `enemy.gd` (enemy types 1 to 8), `hud.gd` (HUD). |

The Python tools are the easiest starting point for the converter of an MSX build: they already produce maps,
tiles, sprites and wave tables as plain files.

## 3. The original in numbers

| | Amiga original | MSX2 / MSX2+ (target) |
|---|---|---|
| CPU | 68000, 7.09 MHz (PAL) | Z80, 3.58 MHz (turboR: R800 7.16 MHz) |
| Frame | 50 Hz, everything runs once per frame | 50 or 60 Hz depending on the machine |
| Picture | 320 x 240 playfield, 5 bitplanes (32 colours, levels 1 to 3) or 4 bitplanes (16 colours, levels 4 and 5) | 256 x 212, 4 bit (screen 5), 16 colours of a 512 colour palette |
| Scrolling | pointer + 16 pixel column redraw | V9958 hardware horizontal scroll (MSX2+/turboR); V9938 only has the display adjust, see section 5 |
| Sprites | 8 hardware sprites (HUD bar, highlight), everything else is blitter "bobs" restored from a clean copy | 32 sprites, 8 per line (sprite mode 2), VDP command engine for bitmap copies |
| Palette tricks | Copper gradient on the sky / water (colour 10 and others, changed per line) | line interrupt (R#19) can change palette entries per line |
| Sound | 4 channel 8 bit DMA, music is TFMX | PSG (3 voices) plus optionally SCC or MSX-MUSIC (FM) |
| RAM | 512 KB chip RAM | 64 KB main RAM + 128 KB VRAM (MSX2 minimum), ROM on a mapper |

Per-level sizes (unpacked): level files are 180 to 210 KB each including code; the *data* an MSX build
needs is much smaller:

| Level | Name | Map | Distinct tiles used by the map | Tile size | Planes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Garden | 460 x 15 | 356 | 160 B | 5 |
| 2 | Pond | 460 x 15 | 349 | 160 B | 5 |
| 3 | Sewer | 360 x 15 | 354 | 160 B | 5 |
| 4 | Machine | 460 x 15 | 204 | 128 B | 4 |
| 5 | Castle | generated by the level's own scroller (about 1100 columns, only tile rows 0, 1, 12, 13, 14 are used, the rest is black) | see `ScrollerMap` | 128 B | 4 |

Tiles are 16 x 16 pixels. Level 4 and 5 are 16 colours already; levels 1 to 3 have to be reduced from 32 to
16 colours (the sky and water gradients are Copper effects anyway, they are not in the tile colours).

## 4. Choosing the target

* **MSX2+ / turboR (V9958)** is the sensible baseline: the hardware horizontal scroll registers (R#26 / R#27)
  make a 0.5 pixel per frame side scroller cheap.
* **Plain MSX2 (V9938)** can be done, but horizontal scrolling then has to be built from the display adjust
  register (R#18, a few pixels) plus a column wise redraw into a wrap around page pair, or by VDP block copies of
  the whole picture. Expect to spend a good part of the project on this.
* **MSX1 (TMS9918)** is not recommended: no hardware scroll, 4 sprites per line, two colours per 8 pixel line in
  the bitmap mode. A cut down "same levels, different game" version is conceivable but is a design job, not a
  port.

Run the game logic at 50 Hz. On a 60 Hz machine either drop one frame in six or accept a 20 % faster game
(the original's speeds are in pixels per 50 Hz tick, see section 7).

## 5. Screen geometry

* The Amiga playfield is **20 tile columns x 15 tile rows** (320 x 240) with a 16 pixel margin to the left. The
  MSX2 screen is **16 tile columns** wide and 212 lines high, i.e. 13 tile rows (208 lines).
* Crop what you must and keep the gameplay area comparable: drop the first and last tile row (they are mostly
  ceiling / floor decoration or empty sky) to get 13 rows, and the 4 extra columns on the right (the original
  spawns enemies at raw x up to about 350). Positions in the wave tables are in the original's raw coordinates:
  `screen_x = raw_x - 14`, `screen_y = raw_y`.
* Objects enter from the right edge, so a narrower screen means they have 64 pixels less to travel before they
  are visible. Shift the wave trigger column by 4 (or hold the spawn until the object's x is within your window)
  if you want the same pacing.
* HUD: the original has two hi-res strips (top 8 lines, bottom 9 lines). On a 212 line screen use the lines you
  cut from the playfield. The content (section 8.5) is a score, spare ships and an 8 slot power-up bar.

Scroll speed is 0.5 pixel per 50 Hz tick in levels 1 to 4 (one 16 pixel column every 32 ticks) and 2 pixels per
tick in level 5. A new column of 13 to 15 tiles (about 2 KB at 4 bit) therefore has to reach VRAM only every
32 ticks (every 8 ticks in level 5). The tile graphics can stay in ROM and be streamed into the scroll buffer
column by column, which saves a lot of VRAM.

## 6. Data pipeline for the converter

Work through these in order. All addresses are Amiga RAM addresses of the *unpacked* level file, loaded at
`$0A00` (`file offset = address - $0A00`); the per-level table is in `src/amiga/level_defs.gd`.

1. **Disk**: `Disk.1` / `Disk.2` hold a custom 22 file table (`DiskSet`, `tools/extract_files.py`); packed files use
   a simple LZSS (`Tpwm`, `tools/tpwm.py`). Level files: 2, 11, 13, 15, 17 (levels 1 to 5). File 1 is the HUD
   panel / font / small routines, file 5 the player ship module, file 20 the ending program, files 6 to 10 the
   TFMX music modules of the levels.
2. **Tiles**: 16 x 16, stored by row: for every scanline one word per plane (`p0 p1 p2 p3 [p4]`), 160 bytes
   (5 planes) or 128 bytes (4 planes). The colour index of a pixel is its `p0` bit + 2 x `p1` bit + 4 x `p2` bit
   + ..., leftmost pixel in bit 15. Convert to 4 bit packed pixels with a 16 entry palette per level.
3. **Map**: `cols` x 15 big endian words, row major (920 bytes per row in level 1). Low 10 bits = tile number
   (up to 365), `$0400` = draw the tile vertically flipped, and the top bits carry the collision quadrant flags
   used by the ship's wall test (`$4000 $2000 $1000 $0800`, one per 8 x 8 quadrant). Because more than 256
   different tiles are used per level, store the tile number as 9 bits (or split a level into sections with
   their own tile set).
4. **Palette**: 32 words of `0RGB` (4 bits per channel) at the level's `pal` address; the Copper list at
   `copper` changes colours per line (sky / water gradients) and has to become either a line interrupt
   routine or a fixed colour per band. MSX2 palette entries are 3 bits per channel (drop the lowest bit).
5. **Enemy and decoration graphics ("bobs")**: a table of 18 byte entries at `bobs` (count in `bob_count`):
   `gfx.l mask.l width_words+flags.w height.w xoff.w yoff.w hit_w.b hit_h.b`. Graphics are plane major,
   one mask plane is shared by all planes. Convert to a 4 bit image where mask 0 becomes the transparent colour
   (reserve one palette entry for "transparent" by duplicating a colour if a bob uses colour index 0 as a real
   colour).
6. **Player ship**: frame table at `player` (variants: straight / up / down), 4 animation frames each, graphics
   at `player_gfx`. Pods (options) are drawn from the same shot art, see `weapons.gd`.
7. **Shots, items, explosions**: `shots` (weapon art), `item_gfx` / `item_mask` (power-up capsule),
   `ball_script` (enemy bullet frames). The small explosion is built from the wave shot's orbs.
8. **Waves**: the table pointed to by the long at `waves`, 24 byte entries until a column word of `$FFFF`
   (`LevelData._parse_waves`): `col.w type_word.w x.w y.w hp_frame.w script.w ... score_bcd.w fire_timer.b
   fire_reload.b handler.w`. Entries with bit 15 of the type word set are 8 byte *group* headers that point to
   a formation list of 24 byte entries (`decode_formation`). A wave fires when the column counter *equals*
   its column (section 7).
9. **Movement scripts**: types 2 and 3 follow a path of `(duration, dx, dy)` or `(0, frame)` steps
   (`LevelData.decode_path`); the angle table (`angle`) drives polar movers. These are plain data and are the
   easy 80 % of the enemies.
10. **Text and font**: file 1 holds an 8 x 8 font strip (44 glyphs: A to Z, 0 to 9, a few symbols) and the HUD
    panel bitmaps (section 8.5). Story / ending pages are described in section 10.

`tools/export_all.py` already writes most of this as JSON / CSV (`extracted/levelN_waves.csv`,
`levelN_ai.json`). Extend those instead of starting from zero.

## 7. Rules the engine has to implement

Read `src/game/game.gd` next to this list; the line comments there carry the original's addresses.

* **Main loop** (50 Hz): scroll, spawn waves, move enemies, move shots, collisions, draw, music tick.
* **Wave column counter** holds 21 (levels 1 to 3) or 20 (levels 4, 5) when the loop starts and grows by one
  every 16 scrolled pixels. A level ends when it reaches `cols + 1`. Level 4 ends by an end flag its last
  guardian sets, level 5 by its own routine at counter `$44A`.
* **Enemy record**: 32 bytes (levels 1 to 3) or 38 bytes (levels 4, 5): type word, x, y, group, hit points
  (bit 7 = flash on hit), frame, hit box, fire timers, script pointer, score (BCD), handler address.
  Types: 1 scroll locked object, 2 / 3 path followers, 4 handler object (shooters, plants, guardians),
  5 set piece blitted into the playfield, 6 / 7 polar movers, 8 table picture (level 5).
* **Hit points and difficulty** (`$A04E`): easy and practice use `hp/2 + hp/4` (at least 1), normal `hp`, hard
  `hp + hp/2` (at most `$7F`). Aimed shots are suppressed at point blank on easy and practice (levels 1 and 3),
  enemy shot speeds are x0.75 (easy) or x1.5 (hard).
* **Power-up meter** (`player_state.gd`): capsules appear when a formation is wiped out; each one advances the
  highlighted slot (1 to 8, then around). The slots are: speed up, bomb, spread weapon, missile weapon, wave
  weapon, option pods, shield, speed down. The second button spends the slot; in "auto" mode the original's
  rules decide (`auto_wants`). Losing a ship takes one step off every upgrade (current weapon level, speed,
  pods, bomb).
* **Shots**: the player's weapon art and patterns are in `weapons.gd`; holding fire for about a second charges
  a blast released on button up. Bombs: lobbed bomb or two terrain crawlers that follow the map's collision bits.
* **Lives and score**: score is BCD; extra ships at 20000 / 40000 (repeating) or once at 8000 / 5000 (front end
  option); lives are *spare* ships (the ship in play is not counted); "continue" costs one of three credits
  and restarts the level with three ships and score 0.
* **Sections**: a defeated guardian raises the phase event; the screen clears, "STAGE n-m CLEAR / SCORE" and a
  bonus count run, then the wave counter jumps to the checkpoint table entry `<= counter + 40`. Dying restarts
  at the entry `<= counter - 23`. Practice difficulty ends the level at the first guardian.
* **Level flow** (driver): levels 1 to 4 in order; on easy the game ends after level 4 with the short ending, on
  normal and hard level 5 follows, then the ending (hard gets the long version).

## 8. Enemy behaviour: where the real work is

Enemies of types 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 are data driven (section 6.9) and small. **Type 4 handlers are 68000 code**
in the level file: shooters, cannons, plants, throwers, mini guardians and the five level guardians. The
GDScript port runs them in an interpreter, an MSX port has to rewrite them.

How to approach that:

1. List the handlers a level really uses: the `handler.w` field of the wave entries (and of group formation
   lists) are addresses inside the level file; `src/game/vm_symbols.gd` has the engine service addresses
   per level and `analysis/disasm/` in the research repo has annotated disassembly of the engine and handler
   regions (`file11_handlers.txt`, `file13_engine.txt`, ...).
2. Services the handlers call (fire in a direction, aimed shot, shot with velocity, spawn capsule, explode,
   spark, add score, sound, music) are the *same* in every level (`$531E` fire_dir, `$535A` fire_aimed,
   `$53E4` fire_vel in level 1, listed for the other levels in `vm_symbols.gd`). Implement them once.
3. Most handlers are 20 to 80 instructions plus a table of frames or offsets. The guardians are bigger: they
   own several record slots (parts), count damage in their own variables and set the phase event. The
   GDScript runs are verified per level and the debugging tool `tests/dbg_boss.gd` dumps the state of every
   part per tick, which is the best way to see what a boss does.
4. Test every handler against the oracle (section 11) by position / hit point traces, not by eye.

### 8.5 HUD

Top strip 8 lines: 1UP plate, 6 score digits, up to 5 spare ship icons, (second player mirrored), 2UP plate; the
active player's plate is lit. Bottom strip 9 lines: eight plates, the selected one lit with a pulsing red.
Exact bitmaps and positions are in `src/game/hud.gd` and in the research notes §18; the score digit glyphs
are 8 x 7 pixels, the spare ship icon 16 x 7.

## 9. Audio

The music is TFMX (one module per level plus front end). The GDScript port runs the original replayer in an
interpreter, which an MSX cannot do. Options:

* **PSG only**: convert patterns / note data with `tools/tfmx_export.py` (extracts songs and macros), reduce to
  3 voices plus noise, hand tune. Effects fit in one voice that interrupts the music.
* **SCC or MSX-MUSIC** (FM): better fidelity; the TFMX macros that are plain looped samples map nicely onto SCC
  32 byte waves.
* Sound effects are numbered: `0x1D` pick up, `0x14` use power-up, `0x1E` denied, `14` explosion, `30` player
  death, `7` enemy fire (see the `SFX_*` constants in `game.gd` and the call sites in the notes).

## 10. Front end, intro and ending

* The front end (file 0) has title, story pages, options (difficulty, lives, team mode, extra select, 1UP,
  control, sound test) and the high score table with name entry. Port as much as you like; the option values
  are described in the notes §16.
* The **ending** (file 20, 430 KB unpacked) is a script of pages: a 5 plane picture (about 320 x 128), a two
  line text, palette fades, plus its own TFMX song and sound samples. Dump the pages with
  `tests/dbg_ending.gd` (saves the screen after every slice of frames); pictures convert like tiles, the text is
  in the same 8 x 8 font. An MSX build can show them as 16 colour screen 5 pictures with the text typed below.

## 11. Using the Godot port as a test oracle

* `tests/test_sim.gd --level=N --ticks=T [--god --chase --max --col=C --diff=D]` plays a level headless with a
  simple autopilot and prints, every 500 ticks, scroll position, wave counter, enemy / shot counts, score and
  lives. Same inputs, same results, so it is a regression log you can diff against your own engine's output.
* `tests/dbg_boss.gd --level=N --col=C --ticks=T --chase` prints every enemy record (type, position, hit points,
  frame, hit box) every 100 ticks: use it to check guardian behaviour.
* `tests/dbg_waves.gd`, `tests/dbg_init.gd`, `tests/amiga_view.gd` dump wave timing, initial engine state and what
  the original code *displays* (it runs the level's own code with the blitter and a Copper renderer) so you can
  compare pictures.
* `tests/test_campaign.gd` chains the levels and shows what is handed from one to the next (score, ships,
  power-ups).

## 12. Suggested milestones

1. **Converter**: disk to per-level binary blobs (tiles in 4 bit, map, palette, bobs, waves, scripts, text).
2. **Scroller**: one level's background at 50 Hz with the Copper gradient replaced by line interrupts.
3. **Ship**: movement, wall deaths, the 4 animation variants, shots (default weapon), HUD.
4. **Waves and data driven enemies**: spawn timeline of level 1 matches the oracle.
5. **Power-ups and weapons**: capsule drops, meter, all weapons, pods, bombs, shield.
6. **Handlers** of level 1, then the guardian; repeat for levels 2 to 5 (levels 4 and 5 are 16 colours already
   and have their own boss room / generated background, do them last).
7. **Flow**: sections, lives, continue, difficulty, high scores, ending.
8. **Audio**: PSG first, better chips later.

## 13. Pitfalls found the hard way

* The wave trigger is an exact equality of the counter, not "greater or equal": jumping the counter skips
  waves on purpose.
* Level 1 to 3 handlers test the player's shots *inline* against the original's shot list; if you keep
  shots in a different structure, give the handlers a function with the same semantics (box test, shot is
  used up, bomb and blast tests).
* Hit point bytes of levels 4 and 5 keep only 6 bits of hit points (bit 7 flash, bit 6 free); guardians there
  count damage themselves (they read `63 - (hp & $3F)` each tick and reset the byte).
* Level 5 has no map in the file: the background is produced by running the level's own scroller; capture the
  tile writes once in the converter.
* Level 4's guardian hall replaces the screen with a second tile map (`$31808`, 30 x 15) scrolled by the scene
  routine at `$5560`; the guardian's body is part of that picture.
* The first column of a level is drawn before the loop starts: the wave counter starts at 20 / 21, so a wave
  for column 21 appears immediately.
